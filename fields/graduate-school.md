# AI for graduate school

Study areas: qualifying exams and thesis defense · research reading, methods and oral examination

## What graduate school study asks of you

Graduate study shifts from learning answers to judging evidence: what a paper actually shows, how its method limits the claim and where your own argument is weakest. Oral exams test whether you can say this clearly under questioning. Ace can quiz you on a paper on screen and then challenge one inference at a time.

## A study workflow

Read the source before summarizing it. Build an argument map, identify limits and rehearse explaining your method out loud.

## A prompt to try

> Ask me to distinguish this paper’s question, method, evidence and limitations. Then challenge one inference.

**How can I prepare for an oral exam with AI?** Explain your work out loud and ask Ace to interrupt with one hard question at a time. Note the questions you could not answer and go back to the sources, not to the AI, for the evidence.

## AI for reading research papers

Focus: research methods and critical reading.

**Workflow.** Read the methods and limitations before accepting a conclusion, and tie each summary statement to the source.

**A prompt to try.**

> Separate this paper’s question, method, evidence and limitations. Ask which inference goes beyond the evidence.

**Where reading research papers usually goes wrong.** Readers often accept the abstract’s claim without checking whether the method supports it. Read the question, the method and the main figure before the conclusion, then ask what the study cannot show. With the paper on screen, ask Ace to question your summary and point to the section behind each claim.

**Can AI summarize papers for me?** It can help, but summaries can miss limits or misstate results. Read the paper yourself, ask Ace to check your summary against the text and cite the paper, not the AI.

## AI for qualifying exam study

Focus: graduate oral examination.

**Workflow.** Explain the core concept aloud, defend assumptions and record the questions you could not answer.

**A prompt to try.**

> Act as an examiner on this topic list. Ask one conceptual question, then challenge an assumption in my answer.

**Where qualifying exam prep usually goes wrong.** Preparation goes wrong when you rehearse what you know instead of what you will be asked. Committees probe the edges: alternative explanations, limitations and foundations. Practise answering out loud under interruption. Ask Ace to question you on your reading list one hard question at a time.

**How should I practise for an oral exam?** Explain a topic out loud for a few minutes, then ask Ace to interrupt with a challenging question. Note what you could not answer and return to your sources.
