# AI for public health school

Study areas: CPH and comprehensive exams · epidemiology, biostatistics and population health

## What public health study asks of you

Public health questions often turn on the study design: who was sampled, what was compared and what can be concluded. Many wrong answers come from over-reading a result. Name the design and the population first, then the measure and its limits. Ace can ask you to spot bias and confounding before it explains the problem.

## A study workflow

State the population and study design, calculate the measure with units and discuss the limits of the inference.

## A prompt to try

> Ask me to identify the study design, possible bias and measure of association in this epidemiology problem.

**How do I avoid over-interpreting study results?** State the design, the population and the comparison before reading the result. Then ask Ace what the study cannot show. Check definitions and formulas against your course materials.

## Epidemiology AI tutor

Focus: study design and bias.

**Workflow.** State the population, exposure and outcome before classifying the design or interpreting an association.

**A prompt to try.**

> Give me a short study description. Ask me to identify the design and one plausible source of bias.

**Where epidemiology problems usually go wrong.** Problems go wrong when the design is misidentified, because the design decides which measure and which conclusions are valid. Name the design first: cohort, case-control, cross-sectional or trial. Then name the likeliest bias. Ask Ace for a problem from your lesson and have it question your design call before you calculate.

**How do I tell study designs apart?** Ask how participants were selected and when exposure and outcome were measured. Ask Ace to quiz you with short study descriptions and check definitions in your course text.

## Biostatistics AI tutor

Focus: statistical interpretation.

**Workflow.** Identify the quantity being estimated, interpret uncertainty and keep association separate from causation.

**A prompt to try.**

> Use this course example to quiz me on the estimate, uncertainty and what the result does not establish.

**Where biostatistics usually goes wrong.** Interpretation errors are more common than arithmetic ones: reading a p-value as the chance the result is true, or treating a confidence interval as a range of individual values. Say in plain words what each number means. Ask Ace to quiz you on interpretation from your lesson, and check definitions in your course text.

**Can AI do my statistics homework?** Use it to understand, not to submit. Work problems yourself, then ask Ace to explain what each result means. Follow your course’s policy on AI for graded work.
