# AI for medical residency

Study areas: USMLE Step 3, in-training and board study · specialty learning, clinical reasoning and rounds practice

## What residency study asks of you

Residency study is squeezed between shifts, so short, focused practice beats long sessions. Board-style questions and rounds both reward a tight presentation and a prioritized differential. Ace can rehearse a de-identified teaching case with you and ask you to justify your plan. Keep real patient information out of study prompts.

## A study workflow

Practise a clear presentation, prioritize discriminating evidence and review the teaching source. Keep real patient information out of public study prompts.

## A prompt to try

> Use this de-identified teaching case to rehearse a concise presentation. Ask me to justify the differential and identify missing information.

**How can I study in short gaps between shifts?** Pick one teaching topic, ask Ace for three focused questions and answer out loud. Note what you missed and review it with your program’s references later. Use only de-identified material.

## AI for medical board study

Focus: specialty coursework review.

**Workflow.** Focus on the discriminating evidence and review the teaching rationale with approved specialty sources.

**A prompt to try.**

> Use this de-identified teaching topic to ask an original board-style question. Let me justify the answer first.

**Where board study usually goes wrong.** Board study goes wrong when time goes to comfortable topics. Use your in-training results or question performance to choose topics, then review the reasoning behind every miss, not just the answer. Ask Ace for short sets of original questions from your notes on those topics, and confirm management with current references.

**Is Ace affiliated with any medical board?** No. Ace writes original practice from your material and has no affiliation with any board or question bank. Check management against current, approved references.

## AI for clinical case presentations

Focus: educational rounds practice.

**Workflow.** Organize the teaching presentation around the main question, important evidence and unresolved gaps.

**A prompt to try.**

> Help me rehearse this de-identified teaching case in one minute. Ask what relevant information I left out.

**Where case presentations usually go wrong.** Presentations go wrong when they lose the thread: details out of order, findings without interpretation, or an assessment that does not lead to the plan. Use a consistent structure and end with a clear assessment and plan. Practise out loud with a de-identified teaching case and ask Ace what a listener would find unclear.

**How long should a case presentation be?** Follow your team’s expectations; many prefer brief. Practise a short version first, then ask Ace which details could be cut without losing the assessment.
